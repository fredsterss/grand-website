Grace is a companion who sits on your mom's counter. She talks with her, helps her through the day, and keeps her closer to the people who love her.

The worry

It isn't that anything is wrong. She's fine. She says she's fine.

But she lives alone and you've started noticing how much she wants to stay on the phone. You can tell she needs someone to speak with during the day.

"My mom did not want to be waited on. So that was another challenge that my friend was like, well, she doesn't really need me to do anything… she just wants to sit and talk."

- Daughter of a 92-year-old woman living alone

"We're far away. It's just a lot of guilting the rest of the siblings to do more."

- Son of a woman with dementia

"I worry about her being lonely, especially since my dad died."

- Son of a woman in her early 90s who lives two hours away

What Grace does

Company first. Everything else follows.

Grace keeps her company

Real conversation - Grace will engage with her about her garden, the grandkids, that story about the summer of 1974 again.

A memory for the small things - she remembers what your mom told her yesterday and asks how it went, notices when she's quiet, says good morning without being asked.

No pretending - she isn't a person and never will be. But there's a friendly voice in the kitchen now, and it isn't just the radio.

She makes the day easier

Reminders for book club, the trash, spoken out loud without a phone. Just ask her once and she'll remember to bring it up at the right time.

Calendar help with appointments, friends who will be stopping by, and anything else that she needs help keeping track of.

Answers, hands-free. The weather. Whether the pharmacy is open. How long to boil an egg. What year that film came out.

She brings people closer

Calls by name - “Grace, call my sister.” No dialling, no touchscreen, no hunting for the phone.

Texts in her own words - she sends the message your mom dictates, the way she'd say it.

Gentle nudges - “You haven't spoken to Margaret in a while. Want me to try her?”

How Grace works

Companion. Helper. Family connector.

Grace isn't a screen full of apps or a speaker waiting for a command. She's a presence in the kitchen.

You say her name, or you just start talking, and she's there.

No phone. No buttons to learn.

Just talk. No phone to find, no app to open.
Loud and clear. Built for hearing aids and hard-of-hearing ears, with a mic sensitive enough for a quiet voice.
Sleeps in the dark. No glow, no standby light, nothing to unplug at bedtime.
Plugs in and works. One cable. Set up by you, from your phone, before you even hand it over.

Let's be clear

What Grace isn't.

  • Not a camera. Grace has no camera and never will.
  • Not a spy. Your mom knows exactly what's shared with you, and she can turn any of it off.
  • Not a medical device. Grace doesn't diagnose anything or give medical advice.
  • Not a replacement for you. She's the reason the calls happen, not a substitute for them.
  • Not a secret. We won't help you install something in your mother's home that she doesn't understand and agree to. Grace only works if she wants Grace there.
